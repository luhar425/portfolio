Creating this artifact helped me connect neural network terminology with the calculations that produce a prediction. The architecture diagram shows how inputs move through hidden layers, while the neuron example makes the roles of weights, bias, and activation functions easier to explain. Comparing the Playground results also clarified why model performance depends on the data and the training setup. The noisy circle run had a test loss of 0.142, compared with 0.010 in the baseline run. The larger spiral model improved from 0.451 at 200 epochs to 0.032 at 1,000 epochs, illustrating the effect of additional training. These are exploratory observations because random initialization and other settings can influence results. Building the presentation required translating equations and recorded measurements into visuals that a reader can inspect. I also learned to distinguish training loss from test loss and to report rounded values carefully. In future work, I would repeat each configuration across several seeds. This approach supports clearer, more responsible communication of machine learning results in a professional portfolio.
