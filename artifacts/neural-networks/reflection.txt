My main design decision was to organize the six neural network components by their primary function: architecture, learned parameters, signal transformation, the training objective, and parameter updates. This structure connects the definitions to a prediction and its training cycle. The worked neuron example makes the calculations inspectable, while the architecture diagram shows how signals pass between layers.

I revised the artifact to explain these choices more explicitly. I added a classification table with a reason for each placement and a separate training loop connecting predictions, loss, gradients, and updates. I also checked the parameter calculation: the 2-4-2-1 network contains 18 weights and seven biases, for 25 trainable parameters. These revisions make the relationships easier to follow and reduce ambiguity about what training changes.

The experiment record highlighted a challenge in drawing fair conclusions. Seeds differed across several configurations, and the noisy circle stopped at 217 epochs. I therefore placed these limitations beside the results. Continuing the same larger spiral model provided a clearer comparison: test loss decreased from 0.451 at 200 epochs to 0.032 at 1,000 epochs. This connects training effort to performance on held-out data without establishing that a larger network is always better. For future experiments, I would use matched epoch budgets and repeat each configuration across several seeds. In professional work, I would apply the same discipline by explaining model choices, showing the supporting evidence, and keeping conclusions within what that evidence demonstrates.
