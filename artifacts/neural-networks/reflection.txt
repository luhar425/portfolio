Creating this artifact helped me connect neural network terminology with a model's calculations. Grouping components by function clarified the relationship between architecture, learned parameters, nonlinear transformations, the training objective, and parameter updates. The worked neuron example made the role of an activation easier to explain. Checking the dense-layer formula also reinforced why a 2-4-2-1 network has 18 weights and seven biases.

A central challenge was making fair comparisons. The circle trials used different seeds, and the noisy trial stopped at 217 epochs. My strategy was to report those settings alongside the losses and describe the comparisons as exploratory. The clearest training comparison came from continuing the same larger spiral model: test loss decreased from 0.451 at 200 epochs to 0.032 at 1,000 epochs. That observation connected model capacity and training effort to the course concept of generalization. It also showed why lower training loss alone cannot establish performance on new data.

Designing the visuals required balancing completeness with readability. I used the architecture diagram for data flow and a separate training loop for loss, gradients, and updates. This process strengthened my ability to explain how components work together rather than list definitions. In future experiments, I would use matched epoch budgets and repeat each configuration across several seeds. In professional work, these habits would help me communicate model behavior clearly and support recommendations with appropriately limited evidence.
